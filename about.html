How I got here

I'd rather understand a thing by taking it apart.

I'm a B.S. Information Technology student on the Systems Administration track at UMass Boston, working on both sides of the same question: how systems actually fail, and how you catch it when they do.

I started out keeping a retail store's network and point-of-sale systems alive. Unglamorous work, but it taught me to isolate a failure to hardware, network, or software before touching anything, which turns out to be most of what incident triage is.

That led to the university's Network Security Operations Center, where I work the alert queue: investigating phishing reports, running vulnerability scans across campus endpoints, and writing up findings that someone more senior has to be able to act on. Earning CompTIA Network+ in October 2026 and Security+ in November 2026 put formal structure under that work.

In parallel I joined the Cyberpunk Division of UMass Boston's Machine Psychology Group as an Undergraduate Machine Psychology Fellow, red-teaming large language models. It's the same instinct pointed at a newer target: find out what a system does when you push it somewhere its designers didn't intend.

That's also why there's a rack of equipment in my house and a Flipper Zero on my desk.
